# Emotion Circuits in the Brain (2000)
**Author**: Joseph E. LeDoux  
**Affiliation**: Center for Neural Science, New York University  
**Journal**: Annual Review of Neuroscience, 23, 155–184, 2000.  

---

## Core Contents & Findings

### 1. Re-emergence of Emotion & Critique of the Limbic System Concept
- Emotion fell out of neuroscience due to cognitive revolution and reliance on flawed "limbic system" concept (MacLean's visceral brain / old vs new cortex).
- Limbic system lacks anatomical definition; damage to hippocampus impairs cognitive explicit memory (Scoville & Milner, 1957), not basic emotion.
- Emotion can be studied objectively through fear conditioning without solving the conscious feeling mind-body problem.

### 2. Neuroanatomy of Conditioned Fear
- **Sensory Interface**: Lateral nucleus of the amygdala (LA) receives auditory and other sensory CS inputs.
  - Low Road: Direct subcortical thalamo-amygdala pathway (MGm/PIN -> LA). Short latency (<20 ms), fast, coarse transmission for survival.
  - High Road: Cortico-amygdala pathway (Auditory Cortex TE3 -> LA). Longer latency, processes complex acoustic patterns.
- **Output Controller**: Central nucleus of the amygdala (CE). Projects to:
  - Lateral Hypothalamus (LH) -> Blood pressure / autonomic responses.
  - Periaqueductal Gray (PAG) -> Freezing behavior (hypoalgesia).
  - Bed Nucleus of the Stria Terminalis (BNST) -> Pituitary-adrenal stress hormone release.
- **Contextual Fear**: Hippocampus (CA1 and subiculum) projects to Basal (B) and Accessory Basal (AB) nuclei of the amygdala, which in turn project to CE.
- **Convergence**: CS and US inputs converge directly in LA cells (Romanski et al., 1993).

### 3. Cellular and Molecular Plasticity
- Long-Term Potentiation (LTP) in thalamo-amygdala and cortico-amygdala pathways.
- Thalamo-amygdala LTP relies on L-type voltage-gated calcium channels; cortico-amygdala LTP involves NMDA receptors.
- Intracellular signaling: Calcium influx -> CaMKII, MAP kinase, PKA -> CREB activation -> gene transcription & protein synthesis (anisomycin blocks long-term fear memory consolidation).

### 4. Human Amygdala & Clinical Implications
- Double dissociation (Bechara et al., 1995): Amygdala damage impairs implicit fear conditioning with intact explicit memory; Hippocampal damage impairs explicit memory with intact fear conditioning.
- Human fMRI: Amygdala responds strongly to fearful faces even when presented subliminally (masked).
- Fear conditioning models panic disorder and PTSD. Medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) damage impairs fear extinction (Morgan et al., 1993), leading to persistent clinical phobias.

### 5. Controversy: Amygdala Storage vs. Modulation
- Cahill & McGaugh (1998) argued amygdala only modulates memory stored in cortex/hippocampus.
- LeDoux & Fanselow (1999) demonstrate amygdala inactivation blocks learning itself, proving LA is an essential site of plasticity and memory acquisition for Pavlovian fear conditioning.
