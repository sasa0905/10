# A Theory of Human Motivation (1943)
**Author**: Abraham H. Maslow  
**Journal**: Psychological Review, 50(4), 370–396, 1943.  

---

## Core Contents & Findings

### 1. Theoretical Foundations & Departure from Behaviorism
- Integrated wholeness of the organism; rejection of isolated somatic drive models (like animal hunger) as typical human motivation.
- Motivation theory must be human-centered, not animal-centered (critique of white rat models).
- Synthesizes James/Dewey functionalism, Goldstein/Wertheimer Gestalt holism, and Freud/Adler dynamicism ("general-dynamic theory").

### 2. The Five Sets of Basic Needs (Hierarchy of Prepotency)
1. **The Physiological Needs**:
   - Homeostasis (Cannon) and specific appetites (Young).
   - Most pre-potent: If completely deprived, all other needs become non-existent or secondary.
2. **The Safety Needs**:
   - Preference for predictable, orderly, structured, dependable world.
   - Seen clearly in infants/children (fear of falling, loud noise, parental discord) and neurotic adults (OCD, quest for protectors).
3. **The Love and Belongingness Needs**:
   - Emerges when physiological and safety needs are gratified. Hunger for affection, friends, spouse, children, group membership.
   - Distinct from sex (which is multi-determined). Thwarting of love needs is core in psychopathology.
4. **The Esteem Needs**:
   - Two sets: (a) desire for strength, achievement, adequacy, confidence, independence; (b) desire for reputation, prestige, recognition, attention, appreciation (Adlerian emphasis).
5. **The Need for Self-Actualization**:
   - Kurt Goldstein's term: desire to become actualized in what one is potentially ("What a man can be, he must be").
   - Emerges only after prior gratification of lower needs.

### 3. Characteristics & Nuances of the Hierarchy
- Relative prepotency, NOT all-or-none: Average person is partially satisfied in all needs (e.g., 85% physiological, 70% safety, 50% love, 40% esteem, 10% self-actualization).
- Reversals in hierarchy: Creative individuals in whom creation defies basic deprivation; martyrs dying for ideals; individuals with lowered aspiration levels.
- Frustration-tolerance through early gratification: Individuals who were securely gratified in early childhood develop character strength against later hardship.
- Multiple motivation of behavior: Almost all human acts are multi-motivated (e.g., eating satisfies hunger, comfort, esteem).
